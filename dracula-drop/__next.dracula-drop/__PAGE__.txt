e:T514,Imagine coming to a ledge above a wider platform. One descent line passes an enemy on the way to the obvious lower route. A second line goes straight toward a smaller landing away from combat. Read both destinations before making the first move. The larger platform may let you correct a bad landing, but the encounter may use up the time you saved by reaching it. The smaller platform may be faster if you can land accurately and continue immediately. If you cannot see what comes after it, judge whether the saved time is enough to justify that uncertainty.

Find a comparable fork in an early attempt and test the broad route first. Notice where you arrive after the enemy is out of the way and whether the blood pickup is on a sensible line toward the next opening. In a second attempt, bypass the fight if the layout provides the option. Take note of how much time you spend regaining your orientation on the lower landing. Give each trial the same simple aim: keep descending without losing a second jump to indecision. The comparison reveals whether your current bottleneck is movement accuracy, fighting speed or choosing a route in the first place.

A rough comparison across two floors is enough. The value of the exercise is getting comfortable with the decision while the tower is moving around you.

After spending, identify the next room where you expected an advantage. Check if you could stay on your chosen path, clear an enemy more quickly, or leave a bad position before losing more time.
