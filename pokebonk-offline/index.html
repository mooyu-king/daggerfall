FIELD NOTES · 01

Choose a partner for your first experiment

PokeBonk Offline is a 3D single-player survival game built around movement, automatic attacks and choices made between waves of pressure. Start by choosing one of the partners offered on your screen. On your first attempt, give yourself one question to answer: where does this partner's attack land while I am moving? Watch the area it actually covers. A beautiful animation and a useful line of attack are different observations. Pick a route that gives the move room to work while making it easy to see which enemies slip past it.

A partner you understand is more useful in the opening minutes than one chosen from a tier list for a different version. Spend a short run learning how the attack behaves when you approach from the side, when you retreat in a straight line, and when you turn around a group. Keep the partner on your next attempt and change your route. After that, swapping partners gives you a comparison you can actually interpret. If this is your first browser survival game, you can ignore long evolution guides until you can name a concrete danger that keeps ending your run.

FIELD NOTES · 02

PokeBonk Offline controls and movement

Click in the game before using the movement keys. WASD and the arrow keys are documented for this edition. Use the controls screen for abilities and any changes made in your build. Start in open ground and learn how far the camera lets you read ahead. If a turn hides enemies beside you, make smaller corrections and choose a wider loop. Early in a run it is tempting to chase the closest reward. Try keeping your route open first and coming back to rewards when you can see both the pickup and a way out.

When enemies are coming from two directions, pick which group you want to move around. If you keep steering toward whichever gap seems nearest, you can end up zigzagging into a group that was previously behind you. A wider turn makes the pursuit easier to predict. Once you are moving safely, cross the edge of one pocket of experience and then look forward again. Practise this as a separate skill. Do not assume a long trail of experience must be collected in one pass.

FIELD NOTES · 03

Spend experience to fix a visible weakness

An upgrade is easiest to assess when you remember the encounter before it. If enemies reached you from outside your attack's useful area, look for an option that changes coverage or gives you another way to handle that approach. If you could position comfortably but took too long to clear space, compare damage or attack improvements in the terms the game uses. If none of the choices addresses the exact problem, pick one that helps you keep moving and see what the next encounter reveals.

Read the full option at selection time. A move can have its own requirements, and a guide for a different client may describe an upgrade or evolution that is unavailable in this build. Record the wording of the selection if you plan to compare later. After selecting, take a few seconds to see whether the change altered direction, range, cadence or your preferred route. A deliberate choice plus one observable result gives you a better build note than a list of everything you happened to collect.

FIELD NOTES · 04

Prepare for travel from the standalone address

The first launch requires internet. For a trip where you expect weak connectivity, open the standalone game from the player link, find the in-game offline preparation option, and wait for it to report completion. Keep the tab open until the entire resource check finishes. The game opening successfully while you are connected tells you very little about whether every stage image and sound is already present. Test by reopening the same standalone address in the same browser profile after disconnecting. Start a run to see whether the resources you need for that run really load.

Embedded storage can be partitioned by the browser. Preparing the standalone page may leave the embedded player with a different storage area. Decide which address you will use before leaving your connection. Reopen it consistently and bookmark it while online. If a resource is missing, reconnect and repeat preparation instead of repeatedly clearing site data. Check available storage first. A private session can remove cached resources when the last private tab closes, and browser or device storage cleanup can discard offline data later.

FIELD NOTES · 05

Prepare PokeBonk Offline for travel

Use the game’s result flow when you want the run to count toward local progress. Leaving in the middle of combat should be treated as closing a session, since a mid-run resume is not guaranteed. Once the result appears, check the progress visible from the menu. If the build offers export, save a JSON backup somewhere you control and keep a copy that reflects a known good state. Give the file a useful date before making another backup. Keep it separate from the game files your browser downloads for offline use.

If you change devices, check that both are running the intended solo edition before you try to restore progress. Read the import confirmation and make a backup of progress already on the receiving device. Verify the expected progress in the menu immediately after an import. A backup is valuable because the local game does not share a Daggerfall account or synchronize with the original game's account. You can also keep notes independently of your save. Mark the partner, the first upgrade, and the moment your route ran out of space; that is often enough information to decide what to practise next.

FIELD NOTES · 06

Make a five-minute practice run count

Use the first minute to walk a broad route and watch your automatic attack. During the next encounters, make one sweep through a safe row of pickups and see how soon the approach closes again. When the upgrade screen appears, take the time to read one option all the way through before choosing. Set a measurable aim for the rest of the attempt, such as reaching the next upgrade without reversing into enemies. After the result screen, compare the aim to what happened and choose one adjustment.

If the game opens on a phone, consider a landscape orientation and check whether moving and using abilities at the same time feels comfortable. If the browser seems to be receiving your gestures instead of the game, look at the controls and available input modes before starting another run. If the menu works but combat becomes choppy, try the game’s available visual settings or use a desktop browser. Change one setting at a time so that the cause of an improvement is clear. A short practice run is useful even if you switch devices for longer sessions.

FIELD NOTES · 07

Use experience pickups to measure your positioning

Try entering an early encounter with a deliberate plan for collecting experience. Choose one edge of the available space and move along it until you know how the group follows. Then let the automatic attack make a small opening. Test whether you can collect one reward through that opening and leave by the same side. If you run directly into the center to get every pickup, it becomes harder to tell which part of the plan failed. Breaking the attempt into short sweeps lets you see whether the enemy route or your own turn caused the trouble.

When two rows of pickups are available, choose the row with the clearest exit rather than the biggest apparent payout. After collecting it, trace a new path before reentering the group. If rewards on the ground are distracting you, aim for one upgrade and deliberately pass the next group of pickups until you see the movement pattern again. In a subsequent run, try reversing that decision at a comparable moment. Compare whether you reached the same upgrade sooner and how much danger you had to cross. That comparison is useful even if you do not remember your exact score.

FIELD NOTES · 08

Make a backup before changing where you play

Progress problems often become visible only after opening the game from a different address or browser. Before changing anything, return to the address where you remember making progress. Check the partner selection and whatever completed results the menu displays. If the game exposes an export option, make the backup there while the expected progress is present. Save it with a recognizable name and a copy of the source address in your own notes. On a different device, first open the corresponding offline build and confirm that the import accepts the format it expects. Read the prompt before confirming, especially if the destination already has a save worth keeping.

When merely changing devices for comfort, it can be simpler to run a fresh movement exercise first and decide whether you need to transfer anything later. Device testing and save migration are two separate tasks. A temporary practice attempt can tell you whether the camera, performance and touch controls work for you without requiring you to risk an older save. If a file seems missing, look in your downloads and check which browser profile last played the game. Keeping the original export unchanged makes it much easier to retry a restore.

FIELD NOTES · 09

Pick one comparison to carry into the next run

In PokeBonk Offline, you can use a short session to compare two movement decisions with the same partner and opening attack. Choose a familiar area, record the route that kept you safe longest, and repeat it before changing an upgrade. A partner comparison is best done afterward, once you have a route you can repeat.

How do I aim automatic attacks?

Watch their actual coverage while moving and position around that observed pattern.

Can I restore my progress?

Use the game’s export and import options and keep dated backups.

Is this the same as Pokemon Survivor?

PokeBonk Offline is a separate 3D survival build with its own partner progression.

Does an iframe save follow me to the standalone page?

Browser storage partitioning can keep those sessions separate. Verify before switching.
