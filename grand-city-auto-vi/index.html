FIELD NOTES · 01

Begin with a landmark in Sunset Strand

Market Shift is our original single-player city sandbox. The browser build is titled Grand City Auto VI and opens in Sunset Strand, a coastal setting with connected districts and local story tasks. Expect to walk through streets, switch between driving and on-foot interactions, and follow the clues in the mission prompts. It is a standalone game with its own characters, layout and progress. People looking for the commercial GTA VI game should use their console edition for that separate experience.

Choose one location you can recognize again before you leave the starting area. A bend on the waterfront or the meeting of two roads gives you a practical anchor. Compare the direction you travel with the position shown on the map, then return once to confirm you understand the route. Learning this small loop makes a first timed run easier. You can follow the story objective without feeling as though every change of camera angle has sent you into a new part of town.

FIELD NOTES · 02

Market Shift vehicles and controls

The version this guide targets includes eight vehicles and fifteen linked story chapters. Vehicles give you different ways to travel. A quick response helps on a route that needs clear turns, while a suitable work vehicle matters on a task with equipment or a load. Before switching, read the objective to check what the job actually requires. The fastest ride at a straight line sprint may cost you time if you keep missing the turns to your destination.

On a first drive, try a short circuit near where you picked up the vehicle. See how far ahead you need to slow down and how much room you need to turn back after a missed junction. Then try a route with a mix of straights and tight corners. Remember the point where your turn begins to feel hard to control. Use that observation to start braking earlier on the next attempt. Practise in the direction you will use the road for the mission, since the landmarks are easier to recognize from a familiar approach.

FIELD NOTES · 03

Read the task as a sequence of actions

The game presents jobs that can involve carrying something, driving to a destination, delivering or towing equipment, following a timed route or helping another character. Break the mission prompt into the thing you need now and the place where the next interaction happens. Look for the marker that matches that stage of the job. A destination marker and a pickup marker can imply different actions. Recheck the objective if you reach the right district without seeing the instruction you expected.

Arrive with room to interact. If stopping in the middle of a tight turn makes the prompt hard to use, park a little earlier and approach the spot on foot when the job permits it. If the task requires the vehicle, bring it close in a way that lets you leave without a complicated turn. The game can ask you to change how you travel between stages, so follow the current mission prompt after each successful interaction.

FIELD NOTES · 04

Connect the coast and the market

Use distinct districts to break longer drives into manageable segments. Plan from the point where you are now to a junction you already recognize, then find the easiest way to reach the destination side of the next district. Allow space for the final approach. It can be quicker to take an extra junction with a clear turn than to cut across several cramped lanes. This is especially helpful on a timed objective where correcting a missed marker can cost more time than choosing a predictable road.

For a route you will repeat, remember one visual marker before the turn rather than only the turn itself. It gives you time to slow down and set up the corner. Test the route first without trying to set a record if the game offers a practice or repeatable task. Once you can reach the key checkpoints reliably, shorten the time by smoothing one section of the drive. A known route also helps when you return from a side activity and need to find the main task again.

FIELD NOTES · 05

Recover a stalled Market Shift job

If the next step does not appear, stop somewhere readable and check the current objective. Confirm whether you still need to collect an item, enter a specific vehicle, reach a marker, or trigger an interaction. Driving back and forth past the same place rarely helps unless you know which requirement you are checking. Read the position of the marker at your new angle and compare it to the side of the street on which you arrived.

When a timer expires, replay the route with the checkpoint that cost you the most attention in mind. Take that section at a speed you can control. If you repeat the first half of a run successfully, start adjusting a later section instead of relearning the start each time. Check what the game actually retained on the result screen or in the mission log before you reload. The checklist on this page helps you track your plan while you play and does not report the job's status.

FIELD NOTES · 06

Return to your local session consistently

Start in the page if you want the route notes close by. Use the standalone link for a larger view or when the browser gives the embedded frame limited storage or input. Make sure the game you reopen is the edition you played before, and verify progress from its interface. Browsers can separate an embedded session from a standalone visit, and opening a private window creates another storage context. Avoid clearing browser data while diagnosing a mission problem you may be able to resolve from the objectives alone.

On a small screen, rotate if it gives the map and controls more room. Look for a stable way to steer and trigger the required interaction before you attempt a timing challenge. A first unhurried drive around a recognizable section of the city is a useful test. If you plan to return later, note the mission and landmark where you left off. That small habit helps you distinguish a lost marker from a genuinely different stage when you resume.

FIELD NOTES · 07

Build a route for a delivery before you drive

Look at where the job begins and ends while you still have time to plan. Find a landmark near the pickup and choose a road that connects it to a junction you can recognize. Then work back from the destination to see how you will approach the final side of the street. If the marker is near a tight corner, leave space to come to a stop and understand the interaction. This planning works for a route that runs along the coast as well as one that takes you through crowded blocks near the market.

For a mission involving a load, notice whether the game has asked for a specific kind of vehicle. Test the turning space early instead of discovering it at the final corner. If you take a wrong road, check for a clear way to get back to the junction rather than making hurried turns while looking for the marker. A reliable recovery path can save an attempt that initially looks lost. Make a short rehearsal of a difficult approach in free movement when you have the option. You will know what the destination looks like before you carry the task into the area.

FIELD NOTES · 08

Try a driving practice circuit with a purpose

Choose two corners near a junction and one easy straight section. Drive the same circuit at a comfortable pace. On the first pass, observe how the vehicle handles the corner. On the second, slow down a little sooner and see whether your exit direction improves. Compare the same route in another vehicle only after you understand the first one. This gives you a way to choose between the options based on how you drive, especially when your normal error is overshooting a corner or missing a checkpoint.

Plan a way back to the main objective before spending a long time exploring. You can read the route through a series of landmarks rather than trying to memorize the entire city. A waterfront approach, a familiar market corner and a return path to your job form a small loop worth knowing. Pass through it in both directions, since navigating by a recognized landmark is different when approaching from the other side. The city feels easier to explore when you know you can find your way back to an active task.

FIELD NOTES · 09

Work through a timed objective without panicking

Read the start and finish prompt and make sure you can steer before committing. As the run starts, focus on reaching the first two checkpoints cleanly. A wrong turn early can consume more time than taking the next corner at a lower speed. Look ahead for the checkpoint and then for the road you will use immediately after it. If a marker seems to lie off your intended line, check the map and use the next clear approach to reach it.

After a failure, identify the last checkpoint you reached confidently. Start improving where you first lost your prediction of the route. Practise braking, checking the marker earlier or approaching from the opposite side during a separate drive. Then replay the job when you have a concrete adjustment in mind. Use the result screen to compare attempts if the build displays a record. Your own notes can also help you remember which corner mattered without pretending to be part of the game's save system.

Before you continue

Is this the commercial GTA VI?

It is Market Shift, an original independent browser sandbox under the Grand City Auto VI title.

Can I bring friends into the city?

The browser game runs as a solo experience.

How big is the current build?

The referenced build contains eight vehicles and fifteen story chapters.

Should I pick the fastest vehicle?

Check the job first and choose a vehicle you can control on that route.

What if I reach the marker and nothing happens?

Read the active objective for a required item, vehicle or interaction.

Can I explore before taking a mission?

Use free movement to learn nearby roads and landmarks before starting a timed task.

Where do I check if I finished?

Consult the in-game task log or result. The guide checklist is only for planning.
